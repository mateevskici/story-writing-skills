# Poveste de Seară Guidelines


## Purpose

Create Romanian bedtime stories that are emotionally meaningful, easy to follow
when read aloud, age-appropriate, focused, and calmer by the end.

## Core story requirements

Every story should have:

- One central idea expressed as a “What if?” question
- One main character
- One clear desire or goal
- One central external problem
- One related internal difficulty
- A meaningful choice made by the main character
- A visible emotional change
- A peaceful and emotionally safe ending

## Recommended length

Use approximately 100–130 Romanian words per minute.

- Ages 2–5: 500–900 words, approximately 5–8 minutes
- Ages 5–8: 900–1,500 words, approximately 8–15 minutes
- Ages 8–12: 1,500–3,000 words, approximately 15–25 minutes
- Longer stories: divide into parts or chapters of approximately 800–1,500 words

Length is flexible when the story remains focused and age-appropriate.

## Main character

Define:

- Name
- Role or species
- Desire
- External problem
- Internal problem

- Strength
- Distinctive detail
- Starting emotion
- Ending emotion

- Lesson or realization

The protagonist must contribute meaningfully to the solution. Another character
must not solve the central problem for them.

## Character arc

When appropriate:

1. The character begins with a limiting belief, fear, or habit.
2. A problem challenges it.
3. The character tries their usual approach.
4. The approach fails or is insufficient.
5. The character learns something important.
6. The character makes a meaningful choice.
7. The choice helps resolve the problem.
8. The ending shows how the character has changed.

The change should be believable and appropriately small.


## Story structure

### Beginning

Introduce:

- The main character
- Their ordinary world
- Their desire
- The event that creates the problem

### Middle

Include two or three obstacles that gradually become more difficult.

Each obstacle should reveal character, change a relationship, provide information,
increase or reduce tension, show progress, or prepare the ending.

### Turning point

The character's usual strategy no longer works. They must understand something,
accept help, tell the truth, show kindness, or take a brave action.

### Ending


Resolve the main problem. Show the emotional result through action or image rather
than explanation.

End with safety, warmth, belonging, rest, or a familiar bedtime feeling.

## Conflict and emotional safety

Suitable conflicts include:

- Being briefly lost
- Missing a friend
- Feeling afraid
- Making a mistake
- Waiting
- Helping someone
- Finding a way home
- Learning to ask for help
- Telling the truth
- Repairing a relationship
- Caring for nature

Avoid:

- Graphic violence
- Cruelty
- Humiliation
- Overwhelming danger
- Unresolved frightening threats
- Endings that leave a young listener anxious

The story may create curiosity and suspense while maintaining an underlying sense
of safety.

## Scene design


Each scene must have a purpose. It should do at least one of the following:

- Create or worsen a problem
- Reveal character
- Change a relationship

- Provide useful information
- Increase or reduce tension

- Show progress
- Prepare the ending

Prefer:

```text
character goal → obstacle → changed situation
```

## Supporting characters

Use only the supporting characters needed by the story.

For most bedtime stories, one main character and two or three important supporting
characters are sufficient.

Each important supporting character should help, challenge, reflect another
perspective, provide information, offer humor, need help, change the emotional
atmosphere, or make the protagonist's choice possible.

## Theme

Show the theme through the character's choices and consequences.

Do not add a moral lecture unless it is natural and brief.

## Longer stories

Divide a story into parts or chapters when it is too long for the target age or
contains distinct phases.

Each part or chapter should contain:

- A local goal
- An obstacle
- A meaningful change
- A natural pause
- A gentle continuation hook when it is not the final part

## Website metadata

Stories are Markdown files with this frontmatter:

```yaml
title:
slug:
description:
author: Poveste de Seară
updatedDate:
time:
age:
color:
icon:
theme:
order:
draft: true
```

Rules:

- `slug` must be lowercase, unique, and stable.
- `description` should not reveal every event.
- `time` must match the approximate read-aloud duration.
- `age` must match the language and emotional complexity.
- `color` must be `lavender`, `peach`, or `sage`.
- `theme` should be one clear discovery theme.
- `order` should increase for newer stories.
- `draft: true` should remain during review.
- The filename should use the order prefix, such as `04-noua-poveste.md`.
